Station 01 · Pre-Primary

Nursery School

Ages 3 – 6 · three years

Rwanda’s pre-primary framework does not divide the day into subjects. It sets out six integrated, thematic learning areas, taught through practical activity so that children learn holistically — a morning of counting seeds is also a morning of language, of fine motor control, of waiting your turn. Our three nursery classes cover the three national age bands.

The six required learning areas
Discovery of the World

Plants, animals, weather, the village. Science and social studies, learned by looking.

Numeracy

Counting, sorting, matching, pattern — objects in hand before numbers on a page.

Language & Literacy

Story, song and conversation in Kinyarwanda; English and French through rhyme.

Physical & Health Development

Running, climbing, grip and balance — plus handwashing and the habits that keep a child well.

Creative Arts

Drawing, modelling, drumming, dance, dramatic play. Timetabled, not a reward.

Social & Emotional Development

Naming feelings, sharing, settling a dispute, belonging. Everything else stands on this.

Station 02 · Lower Primary

Lower School

Primary 1 – 3 · seven subjects

Seven subjects, thirty periods a week. The weighting tells you the priority: Kinyarwanda is the heaviest subject in the school at this stage, because the curriculum wants a child reading and writing confidently in their own language before English becomes the medium of instruction. English is taught alongside it from the first week.

Station 03 · Upper Primary

Upper School

Primary 4 – 6 · eight subjects

Same thirty periods, different priorities. English and Mathematics move level at the top of the timetable, Science more than doubles, and French enters as a formal subject — eight subjects in place of seven. English is now the language of instruction, with Kinyarwanda and French continuing daily. Everything is aimed at one morning at the end of Primary 6.

Station 04 · End of Primary 6

The National Examination

Five papers, sat by every child in Rwanda at the end of Primary 6, marked centrally and graded in divisions. It is the same paper in Kagina as it is in Kigali — which is why the result travels.
