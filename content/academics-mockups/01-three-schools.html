Academics

Three schools under one roof, from a child’s first day at age three to the morning they sit Rwanda’s National Examination.

Our Approach Nursery School Lower School Upper School The National Exam Beyond Crimson

Children learn holistically — and in three languages from the very first year.

Crimson Academy follows Rwanda’s National Competence-Based Curriculum, adopted by the Ministry of Education in 2015. It is not a syllabus of facts to be recited. Each subject is built around competences a child can demonstrate — and around learning that is practical, hands-on, and connected across subjects.

What that looks like in a Kagina classroom: nursery children discovering the world through play, Primary 1 learners building literacy in Kinyarwanda before English becomes the language of instruction, and Primary 6 students sitting five national papers — and placing first in the Southern Province in twelve of the last thirteen years.

“We do not simply move children through grades. We watch each one — three times a term, in every subject — so we know exactly where a child is growing and where a child needs us.”

Every syllabus we teach is built the same way: a rationale, broad competences, specific objectives, the competences and learning outcomes expected per unit, the content and materials required, the pedagogy and assessment that go with it, and the cross-cutting issues a child should carry out of the room. Eight components, every subject, every grade.

Our Approach

The S.M.A.R.T.
Way to Grow

Five pillars carry every classroom in the school: Service and continuous improvement, Maximising each developmental stage, Artistic expression, Reading, writing and arithmetic, and Teacher and staff development.

Around them sit the things a competence-based curriculum needs in order to actually work in a rural district: daily instruction in three languages, socio-emotional and health support so children arrive ready to learn, a computer lab, and benchmarks agreed with MINEDUC that we measure ourselves against every single term.

Nursery School · Pre-Primary

A world worth
discovering

Our youngest children arrive at three and leave at six, and in between they do not sit still. Rwanda’s pre-primary framework sets out six integrated, thematic learning areas rather than separate subjects, so that a morning spent counting seeds is also a morning of language, of fine motor skill, of taking turns.

Nursery is our largest division and our strongest performing one: class averages between 75% and 88%, and zero repetition across all three classes. Children are fed here — the school food programme runs through the nursery day — and the year ends with a school-wide graduation in caps and gowns, with grandparents in the crowd.

Lower School · Primary 1 – Primary 3

First words,
first numbers

Lower School is where literacy and numeracy are built, and the national curriculum is deliberate about the order. Kinyarwanda carries the heaviest weighting in these three years — a child reads and writes confidently in their own language first. English is taught alongside it from day one and takes over as the language of instruction in Upper School.

Seven subjects, thirty periods a week, forty minutes to a period. Creative Arts and Physical Education are timetabled, not squeezed in — and across P1 to P3 they are frequently where our children score highest, which is how we find the confidence to carry back into the core subjects.

Upper School · Primary 4 – Primary 6

Eight subjects,
one exam

In Upper School the balance shifts. English and Mathematics move to the front of the timetable, Science more than doubles, and French joins as a formal subject — eight subjects in place of seven, still inside thirty periods a week. English is now the language of instruction; Kinyarwanda and French continue daily.

Everything here points at one morning. Students are assessed three times a term against benchmarks agreed with MINEDUC, and by Primary 6 the class is preparing for five national papers. Alongside it, P6 students lead the school’s monthly community service — walking out into the village with teaching, encouragement, and sacks of rice and beans. Academic work and character formation, in the same year.

The National Examination

Five papers.
One morning.

At the end of Primary 6 every child in Rwanda sits the National Examination. Five papers, marked centrally, graded in divisions. It is the same paper in Kagina as in Kigali — which is exactly why our results matter.

Beyond Crimson

All 44 placed

In 2024–2025 every one of our 44 Primary 6 graduates earned a place at a top secondary boarding school in Rwanda. Top performers are awarded scholarships and we go on sponsoring them — 22 secondary students are on our books today. In 2016 one of our graduates posted the second-highest mark on the National Examination in the entire country.
