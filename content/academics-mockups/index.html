Design Exploration

Two redesign directions for the Academics page

Both are modelled on Berkeley Carroll’s Lower School page — poster-scale display type over a full-bleed photo, a pill sub-nav for the sibling divisions, alternating cream-and-colour bands with rounded photos, a division leader’s voice as a pull quote with a circular portrait, and a three-card grid at the foot. Both are built in Crimson’s own palette and typefaces, not Berkeley Carroll’s.

The content in both is real, pulled from CAR-Annual-School-Report_2024-2025: the six pre-primary learning areas, the seven Lower School and eight Upper School subjects with their national curriculum weightings and periods per week, contact hours, class sizes, the five National Exam papers, and the MINEDUC benchmark set.

The structural problem this page has, that Berkeley Carroll’s doesn’t

Berkeley Carroll gives each division its own page. Crimson has one Academics page that has to carry all three — nursery, lower, upper — plus the National Exam. So the Berkeley Carroll pattern can’t be copied wholesale; it has to be adapted. That adaptation is the actual difference between A and B:

How do the three divisions get equal weight?One full-width band each, alternating cream → crimson → cream → inkOne dossier each, hung off a single vertical progression spine

Who is the reader?A parent deciding whether to enrolA parent, sponsor, or partner checking the school against its own standards

One decision you need to make: how much of the benchmark data goes public

The 2024–2025 report is honest about numbers the live site currently doesn’t mention — overall average 66.5% against a 70% target, repetition 23% against 20%, transition 78% against 90%. Mockup B publishes all eight benchmarks with target-vs-actual and colour-codes the misses; Mockup A shows only the exam results and the pillars, as the live page does today.

I built B with them in because for a school funded by sponsors, publishing the standard you set yourself and the distance you still are from it reads as credibility, not weakness — and the story it tells is genuinely coherent (nursery clears everything, P6 is first in the province, P1–P3 is where children fall behind). But it is your call, not mine. Deleting the #benchmarks section from B is a one-block change if you'd rather it stayed internal.

Two smaller notes on the source data

Teacher-to-class assignments have moved since the report. The 2024–2025 report maps teachers to classes (Patrick → P3B, Martha → Nursery Middle, and so on) but the site’s own staff directory has them elsewhere now (Patrick → P6, Martha → Nursery C). I used the site’s directory as the authority and left teacher names off the class rosters entirely — only the three division leaders are named. The class sizes and averages are from the report.

Nursery class naming. The report calls them Middle / Top A / Top B; the site calls them Nursery A / B / C. I used the site’s names and the national age bands (3–4, 4–5, 5–6) without asserting which letter maps to which band — worth confirming with the school before this ships. Also: David, the Upper School Leader, still has no photograph on file, so both mockups show a monogram.

A

Three Schools

Narrative · poster hero · one band per division

The closest read of the Berkeley Carroll page. A full-bleed photo hero with ACADEMICS set at poster scale under a gold swash, the six-item division nav riding up over it as a crimson slab, then the Head Mistress in a pull quote with her portrait on a solid crimson disc. Each division gets a full-width band — nursery in crimson, lower school on cream, upper school in near-black ink — with full-bleed photo strips between them, and the page closes on the three-card grid.

  • Reads top to bottom as a child growing up
  • Photo-led and emotional — best for a first-time visitor
  • Six nursery learning areas as a 2×3 card grid; full period tables for both primary divisions
  • Keeps the current page’s optimistic framing
7 photos · no JS
B

The Ladder

Reference · progression spine · data dossiers

Same visual language, different spine. The hero states the whole journey as a five-rung ladder — age 3 → Nursery → P1–P3 → P4–P6 → National Exam → secondary on scholarship — and then every division hangs off one continuous vertical rail as a numbered station. Each station is a dossier: stat strip, subject weighting bars, class roster, how the week is built, division leader. Upper School carries an explicit delta panel showing exactly what changes at P4 (Science 7→17, Kinyarwanda 27→13, French enters). Ends with the full benchmark scorecard.

  • Answers “where is my child in all this?” in the first screen
  • Far more scannable — you can compare divisions side by side
  • Publishes the MINEDUC benchmarks with target vs actual
  • Best for sponsors and partners as well as parents
5 photos · no JS

My lean: B. The one thing a parent actually wants from this page is to locate their own child on a path and see what the next few years hold, and A never states the path — it only implies it by scroll order. B puts the ladder in the hero and then earns the rest of the page with the period-level detail that makes “we follow the national curriculum” a checkable claim rather than a slogan. A is the better pick if the priority is warmth over legibility, or if you’d rather not publish the benchmark misses — it’s also the smaller departure from what’s live now.
