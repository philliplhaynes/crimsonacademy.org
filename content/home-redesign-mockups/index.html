Design Exploration

Two redesign directions for the Home page

You asked for the home page to line up with the rest of the site. Both directions below fix the same underlying problems — they differ in how far they go structurally, not in which design system they use.

What's actually inconsistent, concretely

  • The hero doesn't match any other page's hero. Every interior page (About, Academics, Admissions, Student Life, Portal) uses PosterHero: poster-scale uppercase type, a gold hand-drawn swash, a near-vertical photo-preserving gradient, and a short 1–3 word title with the full sentence demoted to the lede. Home's Hero.tsx is a different, older shape entirely — a horizontal left-to-right crimson wash that hides most of the photo, a pill-shaped location badge with a border and backdrop-blur used nowhere else on the site, and the full sentence set as the H1 itself.
  • Section headers are inconsistent within the home page itself, not just against other pages. HowItWorks.tsx (Academics) and Statistics.tsx's siblings center their eyebrow+title; Team.tsx and the Mission section left-align, matching the Head pattern every dedicated page uses everywhere, always. Both mockups standardize on left-aligned throughout.
  • The stats strip is the odd one out. Home's version is a plain bordered strip directly on cream with crimson numerals. Admissions and Portal both use a solid crimson Band with gold numerals and the dot-texture pattern. Both mockups fix this — Mockup B goes further and folds it into the hero itself.
  • Cards don't match. HowItWorks.tsx uses shadcn's <Card> with a floating icon box overlapping the photo — a construction that doesn't appear anywhere on Admissions, Portal, or About. Both mockups move to the plain rounded-xl border bg-background card already established everywhere else.
  • A real factual bug, found along the way, not part of what was asked: Team.tsx still says "34 dedicated staff." The staff directory was corrected to 32 earlier in this project, and the Admissions FAQ was updated to match — Home's homepage was missed. Both mockups use 32. This needs fixing in the real file regardless of which direction is chosen.
A

One System

Same sections, same order — rebuilt on shared components

The conservative direction: every section that exists today still exists, in the same order, saying the same things. What changes is which components render it — the exact PosterHero shell (with its slab repurposed as a quick-tour of the six top-level sections, since Home has no NavSection children to point at), a real crimson stat Band, and the shared card language throughout.

  • Lowest risk — nothing is removed or reordered, only restyled
  • Fastest to review against the current page, section by section
  • Keeps two back-to-back sections (Academics, Five Pillars) that make a similar claim twice
B

Editorial Front Door

Same content, restructured — borrows the language, not just the parts

Takes the fix further. The fact strip moves into the hero itself, matching how the About page's dossier cover works, rather than sitting in a separate band below — and there's no slab, since Home's sections aren't sub-pages to jump between the way Admissions' are. Academics and Five Pillars merge into one section instead of two that echo each other. Stories from Kagina becomes a horizontal scrolling rail, like HistoryPanel.tsx, instead of three static cards. The People section becomes one featured crimson panel, matching Admissions' featured-principal treatment, instead of a cream card next to separate white fact tiles.

  • Shorter page — two sections become one in two places
  • More photo-forward — Student Life becomes a real collage instead of icon-free text cards
  • More editing, so more to review — this one changes structure, not just styling

My lean: B. The page is long — ten sections — and A's fidelity to "don't reorder anything" means it keeps two pairs of sections that are making adjacent points (Academics / Five Pillars both explain how teaching works; the stat strip and the People facts both restate numbers already given). B's merges make the page shorter without losing content, and the hero-integrated fact strip means a visitor sees the proof points before they've scrolled at all, not after the first section. A is the safer, more literal answer to "match the other pages" if you'd rather not touch content structure at all.
