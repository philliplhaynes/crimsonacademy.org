Redesign directions for the About page

All three use the real content — same mission text, same six core values with verses, same faith section, same five leaders, same contact details — and all three bring the history content back onto the About page, which is the question that prompted this. They differ in how. C is the current pick: B’s structure with A’s history treatment.

On adding history back: yes, but not the way it left

History was split out to /about/history earlier precisely because it was heavy — eight full-size chapter photos. Putting those back as-is would nearly quadruple the About page's image weight, which is the wrong trade for a rural-mobile audience. But re-exporting those same eight photos as 440px thumbnails costs almost nothing:

WhatImage weightVerdict
About page today (no history)671 KBbaseline
+ history at full size (the naive move)2,319 KB3.5× — don't
+ history as 440px thumbnails884 KB+32% — fine

All eight thumbnails together are 213 KB instead of 1,648 KB — an 87% reduction, generated and measured, not estimated. The full-size photos stay where they belong: on the dedicated history page, which both mockups still link to. So About gets the story; /about/history keeps the depth.

C The merge — B’s shell, A’s history

The Dossier + Milestone Rail

Sticky rail · editorial column · crimson history panel

B’s structure end to end — sticky left rail with working scroll-spy, report-style cover with the fact strip, editorial column — but the history section is A’s crimson milestone rail instead of B’s vertical timeline. All eight chapters scroll horizontally on the crimson field, with the three head teachers kept from B underneath.

One adaptation was unavoidable: A ran its history band full-bleed, edge to edge. That can’t work inside B — the content lives in a 760px column beside a sticky rail, and a band breaking out of that column would collide with the rail and break the document metaphor. So it’s a contained crimson panel: same treatment, same crimson field, same gold years and scrolling chapters, just bounded by the column instead of the viewport.

The two it came from

A

The Thread

Narrative · full-bleed · one continuous scroll

Keeps the current page's shape but makes it read as a single argument rather than five stacked panels. History arrives as a full-bleed crimson milestone rail — a horizontally scrolling strip of all eight chapters — placed between Faith and Leadership, so the story lands where "how we got here" belongs in the narrative.

  • Closest to what's live now — lowest-risk change
  • History becomes the page's visual centrepiece
  • Best for first-time visitors reading top to bottom

B

The Dossier

Reference · sticky rail · editorial column

A structural rethink: the horizontal sub-nav becomes a sticky left rail with a persistent "you are here", and content sits in a narrower editorial column. Opens on a report-style cover with key facts (780 students · 32 staff · 2011 · #1 in province). History is an inline vertical timeline — all eight chapters plus the three head teachers, at dossier density.

  • Far more scannable — you can see the whole page's shape at once
  • Reads like an institutional profile or annual report
  • Best for parents comparing schools and scanning for specifics

Where this landed: C is the one to build. B's sticky rail solves the real problem — the About page is long and currently gives no sense of its own shape — and its fact strip answers the four questions every parent asks before reading a word. A's crimson rail is the better history treatment: it makes the fifteen years feel like a story you can move through rather than a list you read down, and it earns the colour break that B's all-cream document was missing. C takes both.

A and B are kept above as the sources, not as live options. If you want a genuinely different third direction rather than a merge of these two, say so and I'll explore one.
