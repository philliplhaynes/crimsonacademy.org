The main event

What happens at four

Monday – Friday, after lessons

Eight programmes, open to every child from Primary 2 upward. No fees, no trials, and for most of them no kit. Two of them are on a stage, three are on a court, and three are at a desk — including the one that has moved more children than any of the others. Football and futsal run as one programme in two places; coaching runs as Revisions and as Lesson Reviews, which is why the board below has ten rows for eight programmes.

On the stage

Dance

The way an assembly starts

Primary 2 – Primary 6No kit, no trials

Rwandan dance is not something our children learn for visitors. It is how an assembly starts and how every graduation ends. In nursery it begins as clapping games and song, because movement is one of the six required pre-primary learning areas rather than an extra.

From Primary 2 the dance group takes it further — traditional forms rehearsed after lessons, performed at assemblies, at the nursery graduation in caps and gowns, and for partners visiting the school. It has the largest audience of anything here and the lowest barrier to walking in.

Music & Choir

The most-subscribed thing in the school

Primary 2 – Primary 6Chorus: ElizabethMusic: Frederic

The choir leads worship at morning devotions, sings at assemblies, and carries both graduation ceremonies — the nursery's, and Primary 6's on their way to secondary school. It is the largest activity we run.

Elizabeth, who teaches Primary 4, leads the chorus; Frederic, who teaches Primary 2 and pastors the school, leads music. Because both are class teachers rather than visiting specialists, singing turns up all through the week instead of sitting in one slot on a Friday afternoon.

On the court

One pitch · one hard court
Karate

A ladder a seven-year-old can see

Primary 2 – Primary 6Instructor: EugeneMost gender-balanced activity

Karate is the most evenly balanced activity at Crimson between girls and boys, and the only one with a ladder a child can point at. Belts are graded; a seven-year-old knows exactly what she is working towards and roughly how long it will take her.

Eugene, our karate instructor, is on staff rather than visiting, which is why the group trains right through the year instead of in bursts around someone else's calendar. Discipline is one of our six core values, and this is where it is least abstract.

Basketball

Nobody arrived already knowing this one

Primary 4 – Primary 6Newest programme

Basketball exists at Crimson because there is finally a court to play it on. It is the newest thing on campus and the only sport here that almost nobody arrived already knowing — which turns out to be the whole value of it.

Sides are picked by class first, and the strongest players go on to represent the school. For children who had only ever played football, learning a sport from zero in front of your friends is the harder lesson and the more useful one.

Football & Futsal

The one everybody already plays

Primary 3 – Primary 6Coach: RobertFutsal in the rains

Football is the sport every child already plays, so our job is not to teach it but to organise it: inter-class matches first, then a school side for district fixtures. Robert, who teaches Primary 3, coaches.

When the rains make the pitch unplayable the game moves onto the hard court as futsal — smaller sides, a heavier ball, faster touches, nowhere to hide. Several of our strongest players came up through those wet-season sessions rather than through the eleven-a-side game.

At the table

For the children not found on a pitch
Debate Team

Where English stops being a subject

Primary 4 – Primary 6English medium

From Primary 4 the language of instruction becomes English. Debate is where that change stops being a line on a timetable and starts being a voice. Students take a motion, prepare both sides of it, and argue it in front of the school.

It is the fastest route we know from a child who can read English to a child who can think in it. It also teaches the thing a national exam never tests: how to lose an argument well, and how to be persuaded by a better one.

Chess Club

For the children who go quiet in a crowd

Primary 3 – Primary 6A classroom, a board, an hour

Chess rewards nothing that football rewards. Not speed, not volume, not height. Only sitting with a hard problem for longer than it is comfortable to sit with it — which happens to be exactly the muscle a national exam asks for, and almost nothing else in a child's week trains it.

It also sits inside a long local tradition. Strategy games are played on mats all over Kagina, and a child who has spent evenings moving seeds around a carved board already understands giving up a piece now for a better position later.

After-School Coaching

Revisions and Lesson Reviews

All gradesAimed at Primary 1 – Primary 3Own class teacher

Every child at Crimson is assessed three times a term. That rhythm is useful for exactly one reason: it tells us in October which children will struggle in June, while there is still time to do something about it.

Revisions are small-group sessions before each test, going back over what is about to be examined with a group small enough that a child will actually put a hand up. Lesson Reviews revisit a lesson a child did not get the first time, with their own class teacher, in Kinyarwanda if that is what it takes.

It is aimed squarely at Primary 1 to Primary 3, where our repetition rate is highest and where catching a child early is still cheap. It is the least glamorous thing on this page and the one that changes the most.

The week after the week

Saturdays

Fixtures · tournaments · gradings

Weekdays are for practice. Saturdays are when it counts — district fixtures for the football and basketball sides, tournaments hosted on our own court, and belt gradings for the karate group. Parents come, siblings come, and the touchline is three deep.

It is also the day the school stops looking like a school. Sides arrive from across the district in their own colours, our children line up in theirs, and 780 pupils discover that the place they go every morning is somewhere other people travel to.

The one nobody signs up for

Once a month

Primary 6 · out of the gate

Once a month the oldest class in the school walks out of the gate. Primary 6 goes into Kagina village with their teachers — to teach, to encourage, and to carry rice and beans to the families who open their doors to them.

It is not an option on a form and there is no sign-up sheet. It is written into the school year the way lessons are. Every school day also opens together in devotions — prayer, song and a short reading before a single lesson starts — and our six core values are each anchored in scripture and taught explicitly rather than left to be absorbed.

For a lot of our students this is the part of Crimson they are still talking about years later, long after they have forgotten what they scored.

“For even the Son of Man did not come to be served, but to serve.” Mark 10:45
Underneath all of it

Every day

Support Services · one of our five pillars

None of the rest of this page works without this part. A child who is hungry, unwell, or carrying something heavy from home does not join the choir and does not stay behind for revisions.

Meals

Cooked on site, every day

A daily food programme runs through the nursery day, cooked on campus by three staff cooks. It is the single most direct thing we do to keep a child in a classroom.

In the timetable

Arts and PE are not extras

Creative arts and physical education are timetabled from nursery upward as part of the national curriculum — two periods and one period a week in Lower School. What happens after four builds on that, it doesn't replace it.

Getting home

Buses, and why they matter here

School transport brings in children who live too far from Kagina to walk. It is also the reason a child from a far village can stay for an activity at all — without the bus, four o'clock is a wall.

Beyond the gate, the school also runs micro-loan and livestock programmes for households in the village, and students and their families have access to medical and socio-emotional care. Children learn best when they are healthy and whole, and that is a budget line here rather than a sentiment.
